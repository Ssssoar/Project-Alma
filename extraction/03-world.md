# World

## Save structure

The world of the game is divided in two:

- **New Save** — the player's active/current world. This is where they begin.
- **Old Save** — an inaccessible previous world.

They work exactly like videogame saves would imply, so, they both begin from a past branching point.
The old save has been "abandoned" for a long time. This means that it's player never went in to play it again. This abandonment is the reason for the old save being the way it is.

---

## The old world

The corrupted world is:

- barren
- decaying
- glitching
- populated by damaged alternate versions of the new-save cast
- still shaped by the story that happened there

The old world is not simply "evil". Its defining quality is unfinished attachment.

---

## Corruption

Corruption manifests through:

- alternate characters
- clones
- zombies
- enemies unrelated to the established villain
- glitch-like phenomena
- invasions from the old save
- characters becoming exaggerated versions of their underlying traits

The source repeatedly frames corruption as something that happens when one part of a person is left without balancing influences.

## Saints

"Saint" is the world's name for a variety of folk and mythical creatures and people.
They take many shapes. The real world equivalent would be Cryptids or Yokai or other similar things. (except people know them to be real)
Some of them take human shape and are attached to real historical figures, some of them are just creatures or legends, some of them even manifest incorporeally.
Even the human seeming ones are understood to not be human themselves.
Saints despite being rather rarely spotted, are pretty thoroughly well understood in magical study circles (unlike ghosts).
Saints respond to prayer from people, though this is far more transactional than in real world christian belief. People are not "devoted" to a particular saint, but rather pray when they have a specific request to make. It is not expected for people to offer prayer "just because".
Some saints have specific shrines and prayers, some saints are just kept in mind. The ways to "pray" are as diverse and varied as the saints themselves.
All saints however have a particular rhyme, which is how people associate their names to their domain. "Santa Elmeda, la que te muestra lo que te queda" for the Saint of Lost causes, "San Expedito, el que hace las cosas rapidito" for the Saint of Urgent causes
Since not all of them have a physical form, it can be hard for normal folk to sometimes determine if something has been the work of saint.
Saints have a specific range of requests that they will help with. A saint may be the "Patron Saint of Harvests" or the "Patron Saint of Hangovers". Some of these ranges get rather specific and can overlap with each other. Patron saints for specific groups of people are common as well.
Saints are mostly passive entities. They are not really alive in the way that people are, so they don't have grand goals and motivations. They mostly act as helpful forces of nature.
This can make them rather predictable and contradictory at times.
Saints might depend on prayer to stay alive. A saint people don't have trust in might cease to exist. Despite this, saints do not really fight to keep themselves alive.
Below is a list of relevant saints:

### Saint Elmeda, the Patron Saint of Lost Causes
"Santa Elmeda, la que te muestra lo que te queda"
- Saint Elmeda helps people who have lost things.
- Her domain is lost causes. She cannot help people get things that they lost and turn around a situation, since that possibility would necessitate the cause not being truly lost.
- Instead she helps people salvage whatever is left, mourn losses, and move forward in their life.
- She takes the form of a kind, glowing lady in simple, ragged clothing, like someone smiling despite having just faced some disaster.
- Elmeda gives people hope even in her absence: When a prayer to Elmeda goes unanswered, folk wisdom is that this means the cause isn't *truly* lost yet.
- Stories around her include:
    - A farmer who lost all his crops and was about to lose hope, Elmeda appeared and helped him pick up what could be scrapped. The farmer could eat for the winter
    - A lady who couldn't find her son. Elmeda appeared to help her grieve, to help her move on with her life without succumbing to despair. The son was never found.
- Her position as a Saint of grief and moving on despite losses makes her uniquely resonant to the struggle of the Old World. This makes her powerful under those circumstances in a way Vilú seems wary of.
- Saint Elmeda has a shrine in a specific part of the world. A humble little roadside shrine in the new world, that has been corrupted into a huge cathedral in the old one.

## Library of the Miracle Machine

A major dungeon is envisioned around the Scribe's library.

### Surface appearance
- Grand library.
- Tall shelves.
- Stained glass.
- Beautiful lighting.
- Harp music.
- Books appearing inviting and almost magical.

### Deeper areas
- Shelves become overcrowded.
- Piles block paths.
- Ink spills across the floor.
- Whispers and writings from the corrupted Scribe accumulate.
- Enemies include animated books, ink creatures, and ghostly scribes.

### Central chamber
- Vast circular room.
- Corrupted Scholar exists as a huge antique computer terminal.
- Books feed into the machine.
- Cables connect it to the library.
- Endless text scrolls across the screen.
- The corrupted Scribe is nearby in a destructive phase.

### Underlying function
The "Miracle Machine" is rumored to provide an answer to the corruption problem, but the dungeon gradually reveals that it is itself part of the problem.

## World philosophy

Two corrupted forces are explicitly opposed:

### Corrupted Sage
- Change at any cost.
- Bring the lost person back.
- Force a miracle.

### Keeper of the Dead Saint
- Preserve everything.
- Never allow change.
- Keep the pain intact.

Their conflict is described as a philosophical war between reckless movement and absolute stasis.

The corrupted Singer and Keeper can also mutually reinforce one another:

- Singer provides hope without evidence.
- Keeper preserves the environment for a reunion that never comes.
