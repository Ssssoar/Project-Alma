# World

This will be less about locations and more about general worldbuilding concepts.

## Save structure

The world of the game is divided in two:

- **New Save** — the player's active/current world. This is where they begin.
- **Old Save** — an inaccessible previous world.

They work exactly like videogame saves would imply, so, they both begin from a past branching point.

The old save has been "abandoned" for a long time. This means that it's player never went in to play it again. This abandonment is the reason for the old save being the way it is.

---

## The old world

The corrupted world is:

- barren
- decaying
- glitching
- populated by damaged alternate versions of the new-save cast
- still shaped by the story that happened there

The old world is not simply "evil". Its defining quality is unfinished attachment.

---

## Corruption

Corruption manifests through:

- alternate characters
- clones
- zombies
- enemies unrelated to the established villain
- glitch-like phenomena
- invasions from the old save
- characters becoming exaggerated versions of their underlying traits

Corruption is something that happens when one part of a person is left without balancing influences.

## Magic

### General principle

Magic in this world works by attaching itself to the ideas of **promises and expectations**.

Whenever a person makes a promise, expects something to happen, or otherwise attaches meaning to a particular outcome, the background energy of the world moves in response.

This happens constantly, even at an individual level. However, most individual promises and expectations produce such a negligible amount of magical energy that their effects are practically imperceptible. Promising a friend that you'll meet them tomorrow, for example, isn't going to awaken any meaningful magical power.

These small contributions nevertheless form part of the endless movement of energy throughout the world.

Magic becomes more readily available when many people align their interests, expectations, or hopes around the same outcome. The more people who invest themselves in an expectation, and the more meaning they attach to it, the greater the potential magical energy involved.

### Magical debt

Large-scale expectations can produce substantial amounts of magic.

Heroes undertaking quests on behalf of a nation, for example, may receive power through the expectations placed upon them. Their strength is not necessarily something they possess independently: it is also enabled by the hopes and expectations of the people who believe in their mission.

This power comes with something like a **magical debt**.

The expectations placed upon a person or undertaking create a magical feedback loop. Successfully fulfilling the mission satisfies those expectations and closes the loop, allowing the accumulated magical energy to return to its dormant state.

Failing to fulfill the promise, however, releases that energy chaotically.

The resulting manifestation depends on several factors:

- The stakes attached to the promise.
- The magnitude of the expectations involved.
- The number of people whose hopes were invested in it.
- The collective imagination of the people whose expectations were shattered.

The resulting phenomena can take physical form, but they can also manifest in ways that reflect the emotional character of the failed expectations.

In other words, the failure of a great promise does not simply cause magic to dissipate. It can produce a realization of what the people involved feared, imagined, or otherwise associated with that failure.

### Promises and Expectations

Promises are not quite the same thing as expectations, metaphysically speaking.

Expectations are what activates and makes the background magical energy of the world flow. Promises are what gives that magical energy an anchor. To create the magical feedback loop, both are needed.

A promise, oath, or quest made without anyone expecting it's fulfillment does not grant any power. An expectation being created without an explicit promise made makes magic move, but does not create a feedback loop that empowers anyone. This last case is noteworthy, since it contributes to the flowing magic in the world that manifests by itself. Therefore, phenomena like the saints or spontaneous naturally occurring magic is a result of all sorts of unpromised expectations flowing throught the world.

The fulfillment of a promise similarly also has two parts: the expectation may or may not be met, and the promise may or may not be fulfilled. These are two independent states.

The fulfillment of the promise depends on the person who made that promise (the anchor), and whether they can sincerely believe they have done their duty. The expectations being met depend on the people who hitched their hopes on that promise. When both parts happen, the energy is safely released.

A promise unfulfilled with unmet expectations causes the magical energy to be chaotically releasesd onto the environment. This is the most common case.

When the people's expectations are unmet but the anchor believes they've fulfilled their oath, the magical power forces itself onto them. This causes devastating effect on the anchor, usually causing death or horrible transformations.

When the people have their expectations fulfilled but the anchor still believes their quest to be underway, the anchor loses power granted to them since the people don't make the power flow anymore. If the anchor continues in their quest they risk having their own lifeforce sapped by their own insistence, similar to osmosis.

### The crystals

It is well known by scholars: The ammount of magical energy moved by the world's normal operation should cause an ammount of magical phenomena so much higher than what naturally manifests that chaos and destruction should ensue. The reason it does not is also known:

There are seven crystals which act as stabilizing forces in the world. When magical force would be released, the crystal absorbs a part of it. The crystal grows, and the physical phenomena manifested becomes lesser than it would have otherwise been.

Throughout the entire long history of the world, the crystals have grown to be enormously charged. This allows people to draw upon their power. People can manifest magic by borrowing from one of the crystals, which negates the need for big encompassing promises. This sort of magical usage is trickier to handle, but comes with much less associated risk as it is not based on becoming an anchor to a magical feedback loop, rather by exhausting an energy that was absorbed time ago.

Each crystal is resonant with a particular kind of magical energy, corresponding to a particular kind of desire or expectation.  Each crystal therefore has both a physical and an emotional resonance.

The elemental associations of the crystals are connected to the kinds of wishes and expectations they absorb. For example:

- **Ice:** Wishes of Preservation and protection.
- **Fire:** Wishes of Passion, righteousness, and obsession.
- **Thunder:** Wishes Defiance and resistance.
- **Wind:** Wishes of Freedom, movement and purpose.
- **Earth:** Wishes of Stability, withstanding, and endurance.
- **Water:** Wishes of Adaptability and change.
- **Time:** Wishes of Destiny, narrative, and regret.

The crystals are not simply sources of elemental power. Their role in the world's magical ecosystem is to absorb and regulate particular kinds of collective desire.

When their power is unleashed, they can produce physical phenomena, but also emotional ones corresponding to the kind of energy they contain.

### Magical imbalance and corruption

The loss or destruction of a crystal removes one of the world's stabilizing forces.

Without that crystal, the corresponding kind of magical energy no longer has its usual means of being safely absorbed and used. This can lead to an imbalance in the world.

The consequences need not be limited to straightforward elemental disasters. Since the energy involved is tied to expectations and desires, its uncontrolled manifestation can also produce emotional and psychological phenomena.

The complete loss of a crystal has never happened in the new save, but it is part of what accelerated the corruption of the old one.

Not all crystals in the old save were destroyed, so some of them wind up existing twice throughout the story.

## Saints

"Saint" is the world's name for a variety of folk and mythical creatures and people.

They take many shapes. The real world equivalent would be Cryptids or Yokai or other similar things. (except people know them to be real)

Some of them take human shape and are attached to real historical figures, some of them are just creatures or legends, some of them even manifest incorporeally.

Even the human seeming ones are understood to not be human themselves.

Saints despite being rather rarely spotted, are pretty thoroughly well understood in magical study circles (unlike ghosts).

Saints respond to prayer from people, though this is far more transactional than in real world christian belief. People are not "devoted" to a particular saint, but rather pray when they have a specific request to make. It is not expected for people to offer prayer "just because".

Some saints have specific shrines and prayers, some saints are just kept in mind. The ways to "pray" are as diverse and varied as the saints themselves.

All saints however have a particular rhyme, which is how people associate their names to their domain. "Santa Elmeda, la que te muestra lo que te queda" for the Saint of Lost causes, "San Expedito, el que hace las cosas rapidito" for the Saint of Urgent causes.

Since not all of them have a physical form, it can be hard for normal folk to sometimes determine if something has been the work of saint.

Saints have a specific range of requests that they will help with. A saint may be the "Patron Saint of Harvests" or the "Patron Saint of Hangovers". Some of these ranges get rather specific and can overlap with each other. Patron saints for specific groups of people are common as well.

Saints do not have any particular motivation to what they do. They are not really alive in the way that people are, so they lack personal goals or even a strong sense of self. They mostly act as helpful(ish) forces of nature.

This can make them extremely predictable at times, and seemingly contradictory at others. A Saint may support two mutually exclusive causes, simply because they both encompass their domain. A Saint may as well support a cause that is actively detrimental to the saint themselves for similar reasons.

Saints might depend on prayer to stay alive. A saint people don't have trust in might cease to exist. Despite this, saints do not really fight to keep themselves alive.

Below is a list of relevant saints:

### Saint Elmeda, the Patron Saint of Lost Causes
"Santa Elmeda, la que te muestra lo que te queda"
- Saint Elmeda helps people who have lost things.
- Her domain is lost causes. She cannot help people get things that they lost and turn around a situation, since that possibility would necessitate the cause not being truly lost.
- Instead she helps people salvage whatever is left, mourn losses, and move forward in their life.
- She takes the form of a kind, glowing lady in simple, ragged clothing, like someone smiling despite having just faced some disaster.
- Elmeda gives people hope even in her absence: When a prayer to Elmeda goes unanswered, folk wisdom is that this means the cause isn't *truly* lost yet.
- Stories around her include:
    - A farmer who lost all his crops and was about to lose hope, Elmeda appeared and helped him pick up what could be scrapped. The farmer could eat for the winter
    - A lady who couldn't find her son. Elmeda appeared to help her grieve, to help her move on with her life without succumbing to despair. The son was never found.
- Her position as a Saint of grief and moving on despite losses makes her uniquely resonant to the struggle of the Old World. This makes her powerful under those circumstances in a way Vilú seems wary of.
- Saint Elmeda has a shrine in a specific part of the world. A humble little roadside shrine in the new world, that has been corrupted into a huge cathedral in the old one.

### the Patron Saint of the Sailors

- This saint helps those who seafare.
- It takes the form of emerald green seafoam. Because of this it's not always certain when it has acted.
- In a seaside town, attacked and flooded by the Water Serpent General Cai, despite the town being absolutely devastated by the attack and it having fallen under the control of Vilú, every single person miraculously survived the destructive flood. This is understood to be the Saint's doing.

## Ghosts

Where mythical creatures and entities with an influence on the world that is active and explicit, even when light are called Saints, entities with a more passive role in the world are called Ghosts.

Saints are active, willful, and act on the world. Ghosts instead simply are. There's a lot of debate on exactly how conscious a ghost is, since they rarely seem to react to external stimuli. They seem to observe, but it's hard to tell if they ever are actually observing, or if they merely look like they are.

They very rarely present any reaction, and even when they do it's not clear to what extent it is a conscious entity.

Ghosts are like after images or holograms. They seem to just show up sometime, and vanish at some other time. They are poorly understood since they are both a pain to study due to how inconsistently they react, and not worth the effort due to how harmless (if a bit annoying) they are.

Some places in the world are known to be constantly affected by ghosts, with their people being very used to their presence, while others barely ever witness them at all.

Scholarly circles tend to consider ghost studies to be a useless endeavor for low-rate researchers.

Sometimes ghosts will take the appearance of a deceased person, though just as frequently they take appearances no one recognizes. Some argue these are dead people who died long enough ago that no one alive knows them, though as with many other things with them, this is unconfirmed.

One of the few things that is well known and understood about them though, is that they are *not* souls of the departed people who they look like. Extensive studies of souls and what happens to them upon death completely discard that possibility.

### Unknown (to the world's inhabitants) facts about ghosts.

Ghosts are a kind of emotional-pressure release for the world. On a metaphysical level, ghosts appear and react when the land is overtaken by particularly unifying emotions.

Though emotions and longings are strongly tied to magic due to how expectations work, they are not synonimous with it. This leads to ghosts being almost entirely non-magical entities. 

The people of the world's conception of magic is that it naturally occurs as a result of strong unifying emotion. This is incorrect, but close enough to model the magic system and work within it.

This makes it difficult for any formally educated magic scholar to understand how ghosts are linked to emotion: They know for a fact ghosts are non-magical, so they tend to assume they can't be emotional.

The world before the events of the story, with all it's people with their myriad objectives and emotions, all pulling in all sorts of directions is what makes ghosts non-reactive and rare. When the portals to the old world begin opening and the people and creatures from it, corrupted and unified in grief and longing begin coming in, ghosts begin getting more and more active and troublesome; an unprecedented circumstance in the world.
