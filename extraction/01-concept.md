# Concept

## Core premise

The game is a fantasy JRPG, intentionally beginning with a somewhat clichéd setup in the spirit of a classic Final Fantasy-like adventure.

When the game is first opened:

- A save file already exists.
- That save file is password locked.
- It cannot be entered normally. (requires password)
- It cannot be deleted. (requires password)
- The player must begin a new game on another save slot.

The initial game on the new save appears conventional:

- A fantasy world is threatened by an evil dragon / demon king or similar antagonist.
- The player follows a familiar quest structure.
- A set of elemental crystals or a similarly generic fantasy objective may be involved.

As the game progresses, things begin to feel wrong.

- Enemies appear that do not fit the established central conflict.
- Events seem increasingly unrelated to the original villain.
- The original antagonist is eventually defeated or killed in an unexpectedly anticlimactic way.
- Rumors arise about clones, zombies, alternate versions, and corrupted versions of familiar characters.
- The eventual realization is that the strange intruders are characters from the inaccessible old save somehow entering the player's current save.

## Two worlds / two saves

### New save

The world is active, warm, and capable of change. It is a world that can move forward.

### Old save

It is degraded, corrupted, and populated by alternate versions of characters from the new save.

The old save is not simply an alternate universe. Its inhabitants are specifically connected to whoever played on the old save. It is their abandonement of the playthrough that is the catalyst for both the degradation of the old save, and it's intrusion into the new one.

## Throughout the story the player should wonder:

- Why are characters from the old save entering the new one?
- How does their story differ from the one that was set to occur?
- How was the story *supposed* to go without any intervention?
- How can anything be resolved?
- Who was the player of the old save, and why did they leave? **(this one cannot have an answer within the game)**

The source material does not yet define a single complete mechanism for this.

## Story structure (in broad strokes)

Since the saves are supposed to be two versions of the same story, every character that exists in one is implied to have existed in the other.

As such, every character in the cast has an old-save (corrupt) and a new-save version.

The corrupted versions are not merely evil counterparts. They are exaggerated or damaged forms of the same underlying traits represented by the new-save characters.

### Possible overall resolution

The project repeatedly contrasts:

- purging / erasing / fighting the intrusions from the other world.
- integrating / acknowledging / carrying it without allowing it to dominate

Possible implemetnations are:

- A two ending game structure, where certain player actions will take them down one or the other path (whether integrating the old save or disposing of it)
- One central ending but with distinct possibilities for where every character might end up, allowing integrating some while eliminating others.
- A central, singular narrative where an attempt to erase the corruptions backfires, forcing the characters to work to integrate them into the world.
